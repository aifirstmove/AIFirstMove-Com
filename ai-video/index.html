How to make videos and reels with AI

You can make a finished short video with AI in one sitting, with no camera and no editing experience. Write a short script, pick the kind of video that fits what you want to say, and let the AI make the pictures.

See the three kinds in 38 seconds

Each part of this video was made a different way: first AI footage, then an AI presenter, then motion graphics. Each one tells you what it is good for.

Read what the video says

AI footage: This scene was never filmed. I typed a description, and AI made the footage. It's good for product shots, backgrounds and setting a mood. Clips are short, so you join a few together to tell your story.

AI presenter: I'm an AI presenter. Nobody filmed me either. You write a script, pick a presenter like me, and the AI matches my lips to your words. I'm a good fit for explainers, training and product walkthroughs.

Motion graphics: And this is motion graphics: animated words and pictures, with no footage at all. It's the fastest kind to make. Use it for announcements, offers and quick tips. Then add captions, because many people watch with the sound off.

The short version

  • There are three kinds of AI video a beginner can make: AI footage, an AI presenter, and motion graphics. They do different jobs.
  • You don't need a camera, a microphone or design skills to publish your first one.
  • One script can become a wide video for your website and a tall reel for Instagram, TikTok or YouTube Shorts.
  • Every AI video needs a person to watch it before it goes out. Odd hands, garbled text and lips that drift out of step still slip through.

What kinds of AI video can a small business make?

Before you pay for a subscription, work out which kind fits what you're trying to say.

AI footage: the AI makes the pictures from your description

You type a description, called a prompt, such as "a coffee shop owner greeting customers on a sunny morning", and the AI produces a short clip that matches it. Runway and Kling are two tools built for this. The result can look like real filming, but each clip is short, so you join several to build a longer video. It suits product shots, background scenes and mood-setting openings. It is a poor choice when you need readable words on screen or a person speaking to the camera.

AI presenter: a digital person reads your script

You write a script and choose a ready-made presenter, often called an avatar. The AI moves the presenter's lips to match your words. HeyGen and Synthesia are two widely used tools for this. The result looks like a person talking to the camera, the style used for training videos and product walkthroughs. A monthly plan makes sense if you make video regularly. It is more than you need for a single post.

Motion graphics: animated words and pictures, no filming

This is the fastest kind to produce. You supply the words, your colors and your logo, and the tool animates them. Some tools, such as Pictory, also build a slideshow-style video from a script or a web page, with stock pictures and captions added for you. The words on screen are spelled exactly as you typed them, which AI footage cannot promise. It suits announcements, offers, quick how-tos and anything where the message matters more than the scenery.

Step 1: Write a script the tool can use

The script sets the quality of everything after it, so don't rush it. A one-minute video needs only about 130 to 150 spoken words. Write the way you talk. "Our new booking system lets you schedule appointments from your phone" works far better than a line of marketing language.

Read the script out loud before you paste it anywhere. If you stumble on a sentence, the AI voice will too. Split long sentences in two. Spell out numbers and abbreviations: write "twenty dollars", not "$20", because AI voices can trip over symbols.

Step 2: Choose one tool that matches the kind of video

Beginners often lose time here. Pick one tool for the kind of video you chose and move on. Prices and plans change often, so check each tool's own pricing page before you sign up.

Step 3: Make your first draft

Open the tool, start a new project and paste in your script. In a presenter tool, choose the presenter and the voice before you generate, because changing either means making the video again. In a tool that picks stock pictures for you, expect to swap a few in the next step.

The first version usually takes a few minutes to arrive. If your AI footage comes out silent, add a voice separately: a text-to-speech tool such as ElevenLabs turns your script into a voice file, which you then lay over the clip in a video editor such as CapCut. Check that the plan you are on allows business use of the voice.

Step 4: Add captions and music, and fix the obvious errors

This step is easy to skip, and it is where the time goes. Watch the draft from start to finish at least once. Look for:

  • Lips that drift out of step with the voice, which shows more in longer presenter videos
  • Extra fingers, melting text and faces that change partway through a clip
  • Pictures that contradict your words
  • Captions that are cut off or sit on top of something important
  • A voice that is louder in one part than another

Add captions. Many people watch social video with the sound off, so captions carry your message to them. Most editors can create captions for you. If you add background music, keep it well below the voice and check the license before you use a track for your business.

Step 5: Reshape the video for where it will go

A wide video suits a website or YouTube. A tall one suits Reels, TikTok and Shorts. You can make both from one project. In an editor such as CapCut, change the shape to 9:16, move the main subject back to the center and export again. Then check the captions, because they are the first thing to get cut off when the shape changes.

One caution if you post the same video on Facebook: it can add its own captions when the viewer's sound is off. If your video already has captions built into the picture, both sets may show. Keep a copy without built-in captions for that.

Common problems and how to fix them

The presenter's mouth doesn't match the voice. Unusual punctuation and symbols in the script are a common cause. Take out dashes, ampersands and bullet points, use shorter sentences, and generate it again.

AI footage shows distorted hands or faces. Video models still struggle with people in close-up. Ask for people at a distance, such as "a person walking across a sunny courtyard", or leave people out and show objects and places.

The tool keeps choosing the wrong stock pictures. Replace the picture yourself and search with concrete words. "Barista pouring a latte" finds better matches than "coffee shop". If nothing fits, use a plain card with large text. Plain text reads better than a picture that doesn't match.

The finished video has a watermark. You are on a free plan or a trial. Move to the lowest paid plan and export again.

The tall version looks stretched or badly cropped. Go back to the original project and reshape it there. Reshaping an exported file a second time lowers the quality.

Questions people ask

Can I use AI-generated video for my business?

Check the terms of the tool you use. The right to use a video commercially usually depends on your plan, and free plans are often more limited. Read the terms before you publish, and again before you run the video as a paid ad.

Do I have to say my video was made with AI?

The rules differ from platform to platform, and they change. TikTok, for example, asks creators to label AI-made content that shows realistic people or scenes.[1] Check the current rules of each platform before you post or advertise there. When in doubt, say so.

What is the fastest way to make one short video if I've never done this?

Start with motion graphics. Write five or six short lines, add your colors and logo, and let the tool animate them. You can add a voice and captions afterward.

Can I make a presenter that looks like me?

Yes. Presenter tools can build a personal avatar from a short video you record of yourself. Expect to record a consent statement as part of the setup.[2] That step is there for a reason: a realistic copy of a person made without their permission is a deepfake. Only use a face or a voice when its owner has agreed.

Your first video is closer than you think

Pick one kind of video, write a short script and make one draft today. AI video comes down to three decisions: what kind of video, which tool, and what the script says. Get those right and the rest follows.
