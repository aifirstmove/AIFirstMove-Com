Chatbots

What a website chatbot can do for your business

A website chatbot holds a typed conversation with a visitor on your site. It answers their questions from information you have approved and takes their details when you are not there. For a small business, that turns a website from a brochure into something that does work at any hour.

Try it now

This page has a working chatbot. Look for the chat bubble at the bottom right of your screen, open it and ask a question. Two to try:

  • "Where should a small business start with AI?"
  • "Find me a video about writing better prompts."

It answers from pages on this site and can search for videos on a topic. Conversations are saved so we can read them and improve the answers, as our Privacy Policy explains. That reading is the first benefit described below.

The short version

  • A well-prepared chatbot does more than answer common questions. What visitors ask shows you where your website is letting them down.
  • Screening inquiries before a phone call is one of the quietest and most practical uses for a small team.
  • A chatbot is only as accurate as the information you give it. Thin or out-of-date information produces wrong answers.
  • You should be able to read every conversation your chatbot has. If you can't, choose a different one.

What does a website chatbot do?

A chatbot reads a visitor's typed question and answers from a knowledge base, the collection of information you have built or approved for it. Today's AI chatbots go further than the scripted kind. They work out what the visitor means, handle follow-up questions, and can be set up to pass the conversation to a person when it is beyond them. Picture a patient team member who is always available and knows what is in your FAQs, your service pages and your policies.

Your visitor gets an answer at 11 p.m. on a Sunday, and you don't have to be there.

The familiar benefits, briefly

You have probably heard the usual case: answers at any hour, no waiting, no hold music. Those are real. There is a subtler one too. Some visitors who will not fill in a contact form will type a question into a chat window. A form feels like a commitment and a chat feels like a conversation, so you hear from people you might otherwise never see.

Four ways a chatbot pays for itself that people rarely mention

1. Your visitors' questions are free customer research

Every question a visitor types tells you that your website did not answer something clearly enough. If the conversations are full of "do you work in my town?", your service area page is not doing its job. If you keep seeing "what is the difference between these two?", your product descriptions need work. You could pay someone to review your website and tell you this. Your chatbot gives you much the same insight as a by-product, week after week.

Read the conversations at least once a month, and act on what you see.

2. Screening out poor-fit inquiries before they reach you

Not every visitor is your customer. A chatbot can ask the questions you would ask on a first call: where the person is, their rough budget, their timing and the kind of job. If someone wants a service you do not offer, or lives somewhere you do not serve, that comes out before anyone picks up the phone. Your time is protected, and the visitor gets a straight answer sooner than a callback that would have led nowhere.

3. Collecting job details before the first conversation

If your business quotes for jobs, the first call often goes on gathering the basics: sizes, quantities, dates, photos, what is there now. A chatbot can collect much of that before you speak. You pick up the phone with the details in front of you, and the conversation starts in a more useful place because you are not asking the same opening questions again.

4. After-sale help that cuts refund requests and repeat calls

Your support work does not end at the sale. Customers call because they do not know how to set something up, how to care for it, or what a policy means. A chatbot that has your setup guides, care instructions, returns process and FAQs can handle many of those questions. That can mean fewer repeat calls, and fewer refund requests from customers who would have been happy if they had known what to do. This use gets less attention than the others, and for a business that sells products it can be where much of the value sits.

Two more worth a mention

It can answer in the visitor's own language. If your customers speak languages other than English, an AI chatbot can reply in the visitor's language from the same knowledge base, without you translating your whole site. Have someone who speaks the language check a few answers before you rely on it.

It points people to the right place. A visitor types "where is your cancellation policy?" and the chatbot links straight to the right page, guide or video, with no hunting through the menu.

The limits to know before you start

Skip the preparation and you will get wrong answers.[1] Usually the fault is in the content, not the technology. Without accurate, current information, a chatbot can fill the gaps with an answer that sounds right and is not, known as a hallucination. That is worse than no answer.

A chatbot cannot make judgment calls. A customer describing a complicated or sensitive situation needs a person. The chatbot should recognize that and pass the conversation on, not try to handle it alone.

It does not replace your team. It takes the repetitive, predictable, low-stakes conversations so your people can spend their time on the ones that need a human. If you expect it to cover everything, you will be disappointed.

What to look for when you choose one

Before you sign up for anything, check these four things.

  • Answers come from your content. The chatbot should draw on information you have approved, not on general material it picked up elsewhere. You need to control what it says.
  • It hands over to a person. Look for a clear human handoff: something that alerts you or a team member when a conversation needs a person, with the conversation so far attached.
  • You can read every conversation. The record of conversations is how you catch errors, spot patterns and improve the chatbot over time. If a tool does not show you the full conversations, walk away.
  • Privacy is handled properly. Anything personal a visitor types into your chatbot is information you are responsible for. Ask where conversations are stored, who can see them, how long they are kept, and whether the provider uses them to train its own AI. Then say in your own privacy policy that chats may be saved. How to get started without overcomplicating it

You usually do not need a developer. Many chatbot builders are made for people who are not technical.

  1. List the ten questions you are asked most often. Look through your email, your contact form messages and your notes from phone calls. These become the first entries in your knowledge base.
  2. Write a clear answer to each one. Use plain language, the way you would explain it on a call. The chatbot tends to pick up the tone you use here.
  3. Decide what the chatbot should collect. A name and an email address at the least. Add the details of the job if you quote for work. Keep it short, because a long list of questions ends conversations.
  4. Set up the handoff to a person. Choose what triggers it: the visitor asks for someone, the topic is one the chatbot should not handle, or the inquiry is a particular kind. Make sure you are told straight away when it happens.
  5. Test it yourself. Ask every question on your list. Then ask questions that are not on your list and see what it does. Fix anything that sounds wrong before you switch it on.
  6. Switch it on, and read the conversations every week for the first month. The first weeks show gaps you did not expect. Fill them, and the chatbot gets more useful each time.

Ready-made or custom-built?

Your main choice is between a ready-made chatbot builder you set up yourself and a chatbot built for you. Neither is better for everyone.

What to weighReady-made builderCustom-built
Getting startedQuick, and you can do it yourselfA project that takes longer
Technical skillNone neededNeeds a developer or an agency
ControlYou choose what it knows; how it behaves is mostly fixedYou decide how it behaves and what it connects to
Keeping it currentYou update the content yourselfChanges to how it works may need the developer
Good forTrying the idea and learning what you needComplicated processes or a large number of inquiries

Start with a ready-made builder unless you already know your needs are complicated. You can move to a custom build once you understand what you need.

Keeping it accurate over time

A chatbot is not something you set up once and forget. Your prices change, your services change, and you add products. Whatever changes in your business has to change in the chatbot's knowledge base as well. Make it a habit: when you update a service page or a policy, update the chatbot's content the same day. Out-of-date information in a chatbot can be worse than no chatbot, because it tells visitors the wrong thing with confidence.

Questions people ask

Does a website chatbot work for service businesses, or only for product sellers?

It works for both. A service business can get as much from the screening and job details as from answering common questions. Collecting job details before a quote call suits trades, consultants and professional services.

What if a visitor asks something the chatbot does not know?

A well-prepared chatbot says plainly that it does not have the answer and offers to put the visitor in touch with a person. Set that up on purpose. Do not leave it to chance.

Can I add a chatbot to any website?

In most cases, yes. Many chatbot builders give you one short line of code to paste into your site, or an add-on for popular website builders. You do not need to rebuild your site. If you are unsure, whoever looks after your website can usually add it quickly.

How do I know if the chatbot is helping?

Count four things: conversations started, contact details collected, conversations handed to a person, and questions the chatbot could not answer. The last two show you where to improve it.

Start with the questions you already answer every week

A chatbot earns its place when it is given good information, a way to reach a person, and an owner who reads what visitors ask. Begin with your ten most common questions and build from there.
