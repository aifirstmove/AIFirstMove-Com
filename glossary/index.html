Glossary

AI terms in plain English

The words you keep hearing about AI, explained for business owners. Each entry says what the term means and why it matters to your business. No technical background needed.

A B C D E F G H I J K L M O P R S T V

A

Agentic AI

A label for AI systems that plan and take actions with little hand-holding, rather than only answering questions. It describes a way of working, not a product.

Why it matters: Vendors use the word freely. Ask what the product actually does on its own and who checks it.

AI agent

An AI that is given a goal and works through the steps on its own: looking things up, using other software, checking the result and trying again. A chatbot answers you; an agent acts for you.

Why it matters: An agent can send the email or update the record, so decide what it is allowed to do before you switch one on.

AI assistant

A general-purpose AI you talk to in plain language to write, summarize, plan or answer questions. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot are AI assistants.

Why it matters: This is where most businesses start, and one assistant used well beats five used rarely.

AI detector

A tool that claims to tell whether text or an image was made by AI. Detectors make mistakes in both directions: they flag human writing and miss AI writing.

Why it matters: Do not accuse a staff member, student or supplier on a detector's word alone.

AI Overview

The AI-written summary Google shows above the ordinary search results. It answers the question on the page, often without the searcher clicking through to any site.

Why it matters: Your customers may read an AI summary about your kind of business before they ever see your website.

AI slop

Low-quality content turned out in bulk by AI: generic articles, odd-looking images, filler social posts.

Why it matters: Publishing unedited AI output makes your business look careless. Use AI for the draft and add what only you know.

API (application programming interface)

A way for one piece of software to talk to another. AI companies offer an API so developers can build their models into other products, usually charged by usage rather than by monthly seat.

Why it matters: You do not need an API to use AI. You will meet the word when a developer or a tool asks for an "API key" or quotes usage-based pricing.

Artificial intelligence (AI)

Software that does things that normally need human thinking, such as understanding language, recognizing images or making predictions. It has been around for decades in spam filters and map directions; what is new is that you can talk to it directly.

Why it matters: "AI" on a label tells you very little. Ask what the tool does, with what information, and how you check the result.

Learn more: What AI actually is, in plain English

B

Bias

A lean in an AI's answers that comes from lopsided training data or design choices. An AI can favor some groups, viewpoints or assumptions without saying so.

Why it matters: Take extra care when AI is involved in hiring, lending, pricing or anything that treats people differently.

C

Chatbot

A program you hold a conversation with by typing or speaking. Older website chatbots followed a fixed script; today's AI chatbots write their answers fresh each time.

Why it matters: A modern chatbot can handle far more customer questions than the old kind, and can also get things wrong in new ways.

Learn more: Chatbot, AI assistant or AI agent: what is the difference, and which does a small business need?

Coding agent

An AI agent that writes, runs and fixes computer code from instructions in plain language. Developers use one to work faster; non-developers use one to build small tools.

Why it matters: Software you once had to hire out can now be made quicker and cheaper, though someone still has to check that it works and is safe.

Computer use

An AI operating a computer the way a person does: looking at the screen, moving the pointer, clicking and typing.

Why it matters: It lets AI work in software that has no connector, but it is slower and needs closer watching than a proper connection.

Connector

A ready-made link between an AI assistant and another service, such as your email, calendar, files or accounting software. Once connected, the AI can read from that service and sometimes act in it.

Why it matters: Connectors are what turn an assistant from a clever writer into something that works with your real information.

Context

Everything the AI has in front of it when it answers: your question, the earlier conversation, files you attached and any instructions set up in advance.

Why it matters: Most disappointing answers come from missing context. Tell the AI who you are, who the work is for and what good looks like.

Context engineering

The practice of deciding what information an AI sees, in what order and in what form, so it can do a job well. It grew out of prompt engineering as AI began handling longer tasks.

Why it matters: For everyday use this means giving the AI the right documents and background, not hunting for magic words.

Context window

The amount of text an AI can keep in view at one time, covering the whole conversation and anything attached. When a chat runs past it, the earliest parts drop out or get summarized.

Why it matters: If a long chat starts to forget what you agreed at the start, begin a new one and restate the essentials.

Learn more: Why AI forgets what you told it: the context window in plain English

Copilot

Two meanings. Microsoft Copilot is Microsoft's AI assistant, built into Windows and Microsoft 365. More loosely, a "copilot" is any AI that works beside you inside a tool you already use.

Why it matters: When someone says "we use Copilot", check whether they mean the Microsoft product or the general idea.

Custom assistant (Custom GPTs, Gems, Projects)

A version of an AI assistant set up ahead of time with your own instructions and documents, so you do not explain everything again in each chat. ChatGPT calls them Custom GPTs and Projects, Gemini calls them Gems, and Claude calls them Projects.

Why it matters: This is one of the quickest wins for a small business: set it up once and every chat starts already knowing your business.

D

Data retention (and training on your data)

Data retention is how long an AI company keeps what you type or upload. A separate question is whether your chats are used to train future models. Both depend on the product, the plan and your settings.

Why it matters: Read these two settings before you put customer or company information into any AI tool.

Deepfake

A fake photo, video or audio clip made with AI that shows a real person saying or doing something they never did.

Why it matters: Seeing or hearing is no longer proof. Confirm unusual requests, especially about money, through a second channel you already trust.

E

Evals (evaluations)

Tests that measure how well an AI does a particular job, run the same way each time so results can be compared.

Why it matters: The useful test of a tool is ten real examples from your own business, not the vendor's demo.

F

Fine-tuning

Giving an existing AI model extra training on a specific set of examples so it leans toward a particular style or task.

Why it matters: Most small businesses do not need it. Clear instructions and your own documents usually get you there at far lower cost.

G

Generative AI

AI that creates new content, such as text, images, audio, video or code, in response to a request. It is the kind of AI behind ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini.

Why it matters: This is the AI most people mean today, and the kind you can put to work without technical skills.

GEO (generative engine optimization)

Shaping your website and online presence so AI assistants and AI search results mention and recommend your business. Also called AEO, for answer engine optimization. It is the AI-era cousin of SEO.

Why it matters: You will be pitched GEO services. The basics are unglamorous: clear, accurate, specific information about what you do and where.

GPT

Short for generative pre-trained transformer, a type of AI model. OpenAI uses the letters in its product names, which is why many people say "GPT" or "ChatGPT" to mean AI chat in general.

Why it matters: ChatGPT is one product among several. The others are worth a look before you pay.

Guardrails

Rules and limits built into an AI system to stop it from doing or saying things it should not.

Why it matters: Any AI that talks to your customers needs guardrails you understand: what it may answer, what it may promise and when it hands over to a person.

H

Hallucination

When an AI states something false as though it were fact: an invented figure, a quote nobody said, a source that does not exist. It is not lying; it is producing a plausible-sounding answer without knowing it is wrong.

Why it matters: Check names, numbers, dates, quotes and legal or financial claims before they leave your business.

Harness

The software wrapped around an AI model that makes it useful: the instructions it starts with, the tools it can use, the memory it keeps and the limits on what it may do. The model is the engine; the harness is the rest of the car.

Why it matters: Two products built on the same model can behave very differently. When you compare tools, you are often comparing harnesses.

Human in the loop

A setup where a person reviews or approves the AI's work before it takes effect.

Why it matters: For anything that reaches a customer, moves money or cannot be undone, keep a person in the loop until the AI has earned your trust.

I

Image generator (text-to-image)

An AI that makes a picture from a written description.

Why it matters: Good for social graphics and concept pictures. Look closely for errors, and never present a generated image as a real photo of your work, premises or people.

J

Jailbreak

Wording designed to trick an AI into ignoring its own rules.

Why it matters: If you put an AI in front of the public, expect some people to try this, and limit what the AI is able to do or reveal.

K

Knowledge cutoff

The date an AI model's training information ends. Unless the AI can search the web, it knows nothing after that date.

Why it matters: For prices, laws, news or anything recent, make sure the AI is searching, or check the answer yourself.

L

Large language model (LLM)

The kind of AI model behind today's assistants. It learned patterns from an enormous amount of text and writes by predicting what should come next.

Why it matters: It explains both the strength and the weakness: fluent, fast writing, with no built-in sense of whether a statement is true.

M

Machine learning

A way of building AI in which software learns patterns from examples instead of following rules written by hand. It is the method underneath nearly all modern AI.

Why it matters: You do not need to understand the mathematics. It is enough to know that the AI's behavior comes from the examples it learned from.

MCP (Model Context Protocol)

An open standard for how AI assistants connect to outside tools and data. It works like a universal plug: a service that offers an MCP connection can work with any assistant that supports the standard.

Why it matters: Tools that support MCP can be linked to your assistant without custom building, which gives you more choice and less lock-in.

Memory

A feature that lets an AI assistant keep facts about you and your preferences from one chat to the next.

Why it matters: It saves repeating yourself. Check what has been remembered and delete anything that should not be there.

Model

The trained AI "brain" inside a product. One company usually offers several, trading speed and cost against capability, and replaces them often.

Why it matters: The model you choose changes both the quality of the answer and the bill.

Multimodal

Able to work with more than text: an AI that can read images, PDFs and spreadsheets, listen, speak or make pictures.

Why it matters: You can photograph a receipt, upload a contract or talk through a problem instead of typing everything.

O

Open-source model (open weights)

An AI model whose inner workings are published so anyone can download it and run it on their own computers, rather than renting access from the maker.

Why it matters: It gives more control and privacy but takes technical skill. For most small businesses a hosted assistant is the simpler choice.

Orchestration

Coordinating several AI agents, tools or steps so they finish one larger job in the right order.

Why it matters: When a vendor describes a "multi-agent system", orchestration is the part that decides whether it runs smoothly or trips over itself.

P

Prompt

What you type or say to an AI: a question, an instruction or a request.

Why it matters: A specific prompt gets a specific answer. Say what you want, who it is for and what form it should take.

Prompt engineering

The skill of wording requests so an AI gives better results. Despite the name, it is mostly clear writing: background, a specific ask, an example and the format you want.

Why it matters: You do not need a course. A few habits make most of the difference.

Prompt injection

Hidden instructions planted in a web page, email or document that an AI reads and then obeys as if they came from you.

Why it matters: It is the main security risk for AI that reads outside content and can take actions. Limit what such an AI is allowed to do without your approval.

R

RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)

A method where the AI first looks up relevant passages in a chosen set of documents and then writes its answer from them.

Why it matters: It is how an AI can answer from your own price list, policies or manuals instead of from general knowledge.

Reasoning model ("thinking" mode)

An AI model, or a setting, that works through a problem step by step before answering. It is slower and usually better on hard questions.

Why it matters: Switch it on for analysis, planning and tricky decisions. Leave it off for quick, simple requests.

S

Shadow AI

Staff using AI tools for work without the business knowing or approving.

Why it matters: It is the usual way company or customer information ends up in tools nobody has checked. A short, clear policy works better than a ban.

Skill

A saved set of instructions, sometimes with files, that tells an AI how to carry out a particular repeat job the same way every time.

Why it matters: Teach the AI your process once, then start it with a single line.

Subagent

A helper AI that a main agent hands part of a job to. The helper does its piece and reports back.

Why it matters: It is how AI handles bigger jobs. More helpers also means more usage on the bill and more to check.

Sycophancy

An AI's tendency to agree with you and tell you what you seem to want to hear.

Why it matters: Ask it to argue against your plan, or to list what could go wrong, before you rely on its praise.

T

Token

The small chunk of text an AI reads and writes in, roughly a short word or part of a word. Usage limits and API prices are counted in tokens.

Why it matters: When a plan mentions limits or a price "per million tokens", it is measuring how much text goes in and out.

Learn more: How much does AI cost a small business? Free versus paid

Tool use (function calling)

An AI's ability to call on other software, such as a search engine, a calculator or your calendar, instead of only writing text.

Why it matters: Tool use is what lets an AI do things rather than describe them. Look at which tools a product has and what each one can change.

Training data

The text, images and other material an AI model learned from.

Why it matters: It shapes what the AI knows, what it gets wrong and where its blind spots are.

V

Vibe coding

Building software by describing what you want in plain language and letting an AI write the code, with little or no hand coding.

Why it matters: An owner can now make a simple internal tool in an afternoon. Anything that handles customer data or payments still needs someone qualified to check it.

Voice cloning

Using AI to copy a real person's voice from a short recording.

Why it matters: Scammers use it to pose as an owner, a relative or a supplier on the phone. Agree a call-back rule or code word for money requests.
