Getting your business recommended by AI: AI Overviews and GEO explained

By AI First Move · November 21, 2026 · All articles

When someone asks ChatGPT, Google's AI, or Perplexity to recommend a local accountant or a project management tool, your business either shows up in that answer or it doesn't. Getting recommended by AI comes down to two things: understanding how AI-written answers are built, and making your online presence easy for those tools to read, trust and name.

Key takeaways

  • An AI Overview is the AI-written summary Google shows above ordinary search results. It draws on pages the search engine already has and decides for itself what to include.
  • GEO (generative engine optimization) is the work of shaping your website and online presence so AI assistants and AI search mention and recommend your business.
  • AI tools tend to favor content that answers a specific question directly, in plain language, without burying the answer.
  • Mentions of your business on other trusted sites matter alongside your own website, because AI tools often draw on what others say about you.
  • You don't need a big budget to start. A spreadsheet and an AI chat window are enough to check your existing pages and find the gaps.

What AI Overview and GEO actually mean

These two terms describe two sides of the same change: one is what AI does, the other is what you do about it.

AI Overview is the AI-written summary Google shows above traditional search results. Search for a question such as how to remove a stripped screw, and Google may show a paragraph of AI-generated text before any links appear. That paragraph is an AI Overview. Google wrote it by pulling from pages it already had on record. Your business might be mentioned in one, or it might not.

GEO is shaping your online presence so AI assistants and AI search mention and recommend your business. It is the discipline of making your business easy to quote for tools like Google's AI features, ChatGPT and Perplexity, which give people a direct answer instead of a list of links.

GEO doesn't replace search engine optimization. It shifts the focus. Instead of only chasing keyword rankings, you make sure your pages answer the questions your customers ask out loud or type into an AI chat.

Why AI recommendations are worth your attention

More people now ask AI tools full questions and expect one named answer rather than a page of links. If an AI tool suggests three accountants in your city and yours isn't one of them, that customer may never reach your website.

Small businesses can compete here. Big brands have years of press coverage and links working in their favor, and you may not. But AI tools reward clarity as well as size. A well-structured answer page on your website, backed by a few trusted mentions elsewhere, can beat a larger competitor whose site is full of jargon and vague claims.

How AI search tools decide who to mention

AI search tools either draw on what they learned in training or run a live web search to build an answer, and often both. A few signals shape whether your business appears.

Answer clarity

AI tools tend to pull sentences that answer a question directly. If your page buries the answer in a long introduction, the tool may pass over it. A short, direct answer near the top is easier to quote.

Trust from other sources

AI tools appear to weigh trust much as search engines do, through links, citations and mentions from credible sources. A mention in a local newspaper, an industry directory or a well-known review site carries more weight than your own website saying you are excellent.

Consistent business information

Your business name, address, phone number and category should match across your website, your Google Business Profile and every directory where you appear. Mismatches create doubt, and doubt makes a mention less likely.

Experience and expertise

Pages that show real knowledge, such as specific steps you take, named credentials or clear explanations of how your work is done, give a reader more reason to trust them than generic advice does.

Five things you can do to get recommended by AI

GEO comes down to a handful of concrete actions. None needs a developer or a large budget.

1. Put the answer first

For every service page or blog post, state the direct answer in the first two sentences. If a page is about weekend appointments, open with something like "Yes, we offer appointments on Saturday and Sunday" and give the hours right after. Don't save the answer for the third paragraph.

2. Write around the questions customers actually ask

Think about how your customers phrase a question to an AI tool. They rarely type a keyword. They ask something like "who fixes air conditioners in my area on the same day?" Build pages and FAQ sections that mirror that wording, and give each page one main question to answer.

3. Earn mentions on trusted sites

Ask all of your customers for reviews, not only the happy ones, because review platforms have rules against picking and choosing. Submit your business to relevant industry directories. Tell local news outlets about something newsworthy, such as a new location, a community event or a partnership. Each outside mention tells AI tools that your business is real and relevant.

4. Keep your Google Business Profile accurate

Keep your hours, services, photos and category up to date, and post when things change. Local answers often draw on this profile, so an outdated listing can mean wrong information or no mention at all.

5. Write in plain language

AI tools summarize for people who want a fast answer. If your content uses jargon that needs explaining, a tool may skip it or get it wrong. Short sentences, clear headings and simple numbered steps are easier to quote accurately.

Structured data: useful, but not the starting point

Structured data, also called schema markup, is a small piece of code that tells search engines and AI tools what your business is, what you offer and how to reach you. It keeps your business facts unambiguous. It is not a ticket into AI answers on its own, so treat it as a later step.

Many website builders and content platforms add basic business markup from the details you type into a settings panel. Check your own platform's help pages for what it adds, and use a free testing tool from Google to see what your pages actually show.

The AI content stress test

Before you write anything new, check what you already have. This takes an afternoon and costs nothing beyond the AI tool you use already.

Open ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity and ask the questions your customers ask you most, such as "who are the best [your service] providers in [your city]?" See what comes back. If your business doesn't appear, look at who does and what their pages say.

Then paste your three most important pages into an AI chat, one at a time, and ask: "If a customer asked you to summarize this business in two sentences, what would you say?" If the summary is vague or wrong, that page needs rewriting. Fix those pages first. They are your highest-priority updates.

Traditional SEO and GEO: what changes and what stays

GEO builds on traditional SEO. The table below shows what shifts and what stays the same.

Area Traditional SEO focus GEO focus
Primary goal Rank on the first page of search results Appear in AI-generated answers and summaries
Content format Pages built around search phrases Direct answers, question-based headings and FAQ sections
Success measure Click-through rate and ranking position Mentions and citations in AI answers
Third-party signals Links from other websites Links plus mentions in reviews, directories and local press
Keyword strategy Target exact search phrases Match the full questions people type or ask out loud

The common thread is specificity. Vague content performs poorly in both kinds of search, and AI tools are less likely to quote a page that doesn't give a clear answer.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a new website to do GEO?

No. Start by improving the pages you already have: lead with direct answers, add FAQ sections, and keep your Google Business Profile accurate. A new site won't help if the content inside it isn't clear.

How long does it take for GEO changes to show results?

There is no reliable timeline. AI tools refresh what they know at different rates. Updating your Google Business Profile can take effect quickly. Building mentions on other sites takes longer. Be wary of anyone who promises a specific number of weeks.

Does my business need to be on social media to appear in AI answers?

Social media can help people find and mention your business, but it isn't required. Your website, your Google Business Profile and your listings in reliable directories are the more dependable places to start.

Can a small local business compete with national chains in AI search?

Yes, especially for local questions. When someone asks an AI tool for a recommendation in a particular town or neighborhood, local relevance is a strong signal. A clear local page, consistent business details and real customer reviews can outperform a national brand that has no local presence.

What is the difference between a citation and a brand mention?

A citation is when an AI tool links to your page as the source of its answer. A brand mention is when your business name appears in an AI answer without a link. Citations can bring visitors. Mentions build familiarity, because people see your name again and again.

Should I write for AI tools or for human readers?

Write for human readers first. Content that answers real questions in plain language is what AI tools tend to favor. Pages written only to game an AI tool, with no real usefulness behind them, tend to perform poorly.

Start with the question your customers ask most

Getting recommended by AI isn't about tricking a system. It's about being clearly and specifically useful, in a form that both people and AI tools can read. Your Google Business Profile, your most-visited service pages and a few FAQ sections are where most of the work happens.

Pick the one question customers ask you most often. Write a page that answers it in the first two sentences. Add an FAQ section, and check that your business details match everywhere they appear online. That covers most of the job.
