What does your AI remember about you? AI memory explained

By AI First Move · November 18, 2026 · All articles

Tools and settings checked November 18, 2026

Your AI assistant may know more about you than you've realized. Some tools remember your name, your type of business and your preferences from past conversations. Others, or the same tools with memory switched off, start fresh each time you open a new chat. Understanding what sticks, what fades and who else can see it is worth a few minutes before you share anything sensitive.

Key takeaways

  • Memory and chat history aren't the same thing. Memory holds details about you that carry from chat to chat; chat history is the record of your full conversations.
  • The context window belongs to one chat. What the AI "sees" in one conversation does not carry into a new chat unless the tool has a memory feature switched on.
  • Data retention is a company policy, not a feature. Even if you delete a chat, the company may hold your data for a set period under its privacy policy.
  • Training on your chats is a separate switch from memory. You can turn off one without turning off the other, depending on the tool.
  • You can check and correct AI memory. The main assistants let you review and delete what they've stored about you. Do it regularly.

Four terms you need to know before anything else

What is AI memory, exactly?

AI memory is a feature that lets an assistant keep details about you and bring them into future conversations. When an assistant remembers that you run a bakery or prefer answers as short lists, that's memory at work. The AI wrote a note to itself and kept it. Memory isn't always something you switch on yourself; Claude's Free, Pro and Max accounts, for example, have it on from the start.[2] You can turn it off, and you can review and delete what's been stored.

What is a context window, and why does it matter?

A context window is everything the AI can "see" during one conversation: your messages, its replies, any documents you paste in. It has a size limit, and it belongs to that one chat. Nothing in it moves to your next conversation on its own unless memory is on. That's why you sometimes have to explain your situation again from scratch; the AI isn't being forgetful, it never stored it. For more on this, see Why AI forgets what you told it.

What is data retention, and is it the same as memory?

Data retention isn't memory. Memory is a feature you can see and manage. Data retention is the company's policy on how long it keeps copies of your chats on its own systems, including for a time after you delete them from your view. Each company publishes its own rules in its privacy policy, and the periods vary. Deleting a chat from your history doesn't mean it's gone from the company's systems straight away.

What does "training on my chats" actually mean?

When a company trains on your chats, your conversations may be used to improve the AI model itself. That's separate from memory and separate from data retention. You can have memory on and training off, or the other way round. Personal plans have a switch for it, and business plans don't train on your chats by default. It's worth checking, especially if you discuss client work or financial details. We cover this plan by plan in Is it safe to put customer or business information into an AI tool?

How the four main AI assistants handle memory

How does ChatGPT memory work?

With Memory on, ChatGPT keeps details from your conversations and can also draw on your past chats to keep that picture current.[1] You don't have to ask it to remember something; it decides what to save. You can also tell it directly: "Remember that I work in commercial real estate." To see what it's stored, go to Settings, then Personalization, then Memory.[8] You can also simply ask ChatGPT what it remembers about you. OpenAI notes that the summary it shows may not include every detail memory can draw on.[1] A Temporary Chat doesn't use or add to memory.[9]

How does Claude handle memory across conversations?

Claude used to start every chat fresh, but that has changed. Free, Pro and Max accounts have memory switched on from the start; on Team and Enterprise plans, the owners of the account decide whether it's available.[2] Projects add a separate layer: each Project keeps its own memory, so something Claude knows inside a Project may not show up in an ordinary chat.[3] That's useful for a long-running job like a marketing campaign or a staff handbook. Everything Claude remembers is listed in the Memory settings, where you can edit or delete any item,[2] and you can turn memory off there too.

How does Gemini remember you?

On a personal Google account, Gemini has a Memory setting that learns from your saved past chats. It needs the Keep Activity setting to be on.[4] Google renamed several of these settings during 2026, so the names on your screen may not match older guides.[4] Under a change Google announced on November 16, 2026, the single Keep Activity switch is being replaced by two: one for keeping your chat history and one for letting Google train on your chats. Google says the change is arriving over the coming weeks.[5] Turning history off does not mean nothing is kept: under the new setup, Google says those chats are still held for around two months.[5]

How does Microsoft Copilot handle memory?

On a personal account, Copilot can remember key details and preferences from your conversations when personalization is on. If you turn personalization off, Copilot forgets its memories of your conversations, though you can still see your past chats.[6] On a work account with Microsoft 365, your organization's administrator has a control over the feature; if they switch it off, you can't switch it on yourself.[7] Where it is on, you can delete saved memories or stop Copilot from saving them.[10] Check with whoever manages your Microsoft account for how yours is set up.

What the AI might have saved about you already

How do I find out what my AI has stored about me?

Open the memory settings in the tool you use (in ChatGPT: Settings, Personalization, Memory[8]) and read what's there. It might include your line of work, how you like answers laid out, where you are, or topics you've mentioned often. Some entries will be accurate and useful. Others might be out of date or simply wrong; an AI can save a misunderstanding as if it were a fact. Delete anything that isn't accurate or that you don't want shaping future answers.

Can the AI remember something incorrect and keep acting on it?

Yes, and that's one of the practical risks of leaving memory unchecked. If the AI stored a wrong detail early on, it may quietly use it to shape later answers, and you might not notice. For example, if it noted that you have a team of ten when you actually work alone, its suggestions may keep leaning toward team-based ways of working. The fix is simple: check your memory list, delete the wrong entry and tell the AI the correct information directly.

Does the AI remember what I said in a deleted chat?

It can. In ChatGPT, saved memories are kept separately from your chats.[11] If the AI had already saved a memory from something you said, deleting that chat doesn't remove the memory; deleting a memory doesn't remove the chat. You have to manage both.

Keeping work and personal information separate

Should I use the same AI account for work and personal questions?

Separating them is the tidier choice. When memory is on, the AI can blend context from everything you've discussed. A question about a client proposal and a question about a personal trip might draw on the same memories, which can produce odd, mismatched suggestions. A clean approach: use one account (or one Project in Claude) strictly for business, and either a separate account or a memory-off chat for personal use.

What should I avoid putting into AI memory at all?

Specific client names, financial figures, employee details and anything covered by a confidentiality agreement shouldn't sit in AI memory. Memory is stored on the AI company's systems; it is not a private note on your own device. For sensitive work, use the tool's temporary or incognito chat so nothing gets added to memory. You still get useful answers in that chat; you just don't leave anything behind in memory.

Keeping your memory clean and useful

How often should I review what my AI has stored about me?

Every month or so is a reasonable habit. Memory builds up quietly, and older details go stale. If your business changed focus, you moved, or your team changed size, the AI may still be working from the old picture. A quick check doesn't take long; delete anything that no longer applies. Think of it like a contact record with an old phone number. Leaving it in does more harm than good.

Can I deliberately add facts to AI memory instead of waiting for it to pick them up?

Yes. In ChatGPT, you can type a direct instruction like "Remember that I send invoices every Friday." This is more reliable than hoping the AI will pick up the right things over time. In a Claude Project, you can add a short note to the project's instructions: your role, your type of business, the tone you prefer. Both approaches put you in charge of what is kept.

What's the fastest way to reset everything and start fresh?

In ChatGPT and Claude, open the memory settings and delete what's saved.[2][8] In Gemini, delete the past chats you don't want it to use; Google says deleting a chat stops Gemini from personalizing with it.[12] In Copilot, delete your memories or turn personalization off.[6] None of this necessarily removes the company's own copies at once. Each company's privacy policy says how long deleted data is kept.

Still have questions about AI and your business?

Memory is one piece of a larger picture. Getting it right means your AI assistant works from accurate information, not from stale details or sensitive information you didn't mean to leave on file.
