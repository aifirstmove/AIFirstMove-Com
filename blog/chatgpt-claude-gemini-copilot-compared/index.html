ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot: which one for a small business?

By AI First Move · November 17, 2026 · All articles

Last checked: November 17, 2026

Short answer: If your team runs on Microsoft 365, start with Copilot. If you're on Google Workspace, start with Gemini. If you're on neither, ChatGPT is the most flexible all-rounder, and Claude is a strong choice for work that involves reading and writing long documents. The software you already use matters more than raw AI capability.

Key takeaways

  • The software you already use is the deciding factor. An AI tool that sits inside the app you already open every morning will get used; one that requires a separate tab probably won't.
  • Reviewers rate Claude highest for long documents and writing.[1][2] If your day involves reading contracts, reports, or supplier proposals, that's the edge that saves time.
  • Reviewers call ChatGPT the all-rounder. It is credited with the broadest mix of features, including images, web research and connected apps.[1]
  • Privacy depends on the plan more than the brand. On personal plans, free or paid, what you type can be used for training unless you switch a setting off; business plans do not train on your data by default.[3]
  • None of these tools remove the need to read the output. Every draft, every summary, every answer needs a human check before it goes to a customer or a lawyer.

How this comparison works

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot are the four best-known AI assistants. This comparison looks at them through the jobs a small-business owner does: writing emails, summarizing documents, drafting proposals, handling customer messages, and doing quick research. It is built from each company's published plans and from what independent reviewers report, with sources listed at the end. We did not run lab tests or benchmarks. Prices and features were checked on November 17, 2026, and they change often.

Which tool fits the software you already use?

This is the question that matters most. The AI tool you end up using is the one that removes friction, not the one that scores highest on a benchmark you'll never see.

If your business runs on Microsoft 365, meaning your team uses Word, Excel, Outlook, and Teams daily, Copilot is the natural fit. It sits inside those apps. You don't switch windows; you ask it a question while you're working.

If your business runs on Google Workspace (Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive), Gemini does the same thing on that side. The lowest business plan includes Gemini in Gmail and the Gemini app; Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive and Meet need Business Standard or above.[4]

Neither? ChatGPT is a good starting point. It is easy to learn and reviewers describe it as the better all-rounder.[2] Claude is a close second, and the better pick for businesses whose main use is reading and writing.

What about data privacy?

Small businesses often handle client information, financial data, or sensitive employee records, so this part deserves a few minutes.

The plan you are on matters more than which of the four you choose. On the personal versions of ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Claude, the free ones and the individual paid ones too, what you type can be used to train the company's models unless a setting is switched off. On business plans, all four companies say they do not train on your data by default.[3] The practical rule: before you paste anything sensitive into any AI tool, open its privacy or data settings and check what is switched on.

Some specifics worth knowing:

  • ChatGPT and Claude: the business plans (ChatGPT Business, Claude Team) are the ones that exclude your data from training by default.[3] A personal Plus or Pro subscription does not do that on its own.
  • Gemini: Google states that Gemini interactions in a Google Workspace business account stay within your organization and are not used for model training outside your domain without permission.[4]
  • Copilot: the same split applies. The work versions that come with a Microsoft 365 business subscription are covered by the business terms; a personal Copilot account is not.[3]

Whatever the settings say, leave out account numbers, passwords and anything your lawyer would flag. And none of these tools replaces legal, compliance, or medical advice.

How do these tools hold up for a small team?

Solo use is one thing. Running AI across a team of five or fifteen is a different challenge. You need consistent results, some way to share good prompts across staff, and billing that is simple to manage.

Copilot has a natural advantage in a Microsoft shop: your team already signs in with one Microsoft account, and Copilot is managed from the same admin center. Gemini works the same way inside Google Workspace.

ChatGPT Business gives a team a shared workspace with company context and admin controls for users, usage and billing.[14] That helps standardize how your team asks the AI to handle, say, inbound customer inquiries.

Claude's Team plan includes central admin, shared projects with view or edit permissions, and connectors for tools such as Google Drive, Gmail, Microsoft 365 and Slack.[15]

Who should pick which tool?

  • Your team uses Microsoft 365 every day → Start with Copilot. You'll use it because it's already where you work.
  • Your team runs on Google Workspace → Start with Gemini. Same reason.
  • You read a lot of contracts, reports, or long proposals → Try Claude for that specific job, whatever office software you use.
  • You want one tool that does a bit of everything → ChatGPT is the all-rounder.
  • You're just starting out and not committed to either office suite → Try a free version first. ChatGPT's is a flexible way to learn what AI can do for your business before spending anything.
  • Your main use is writing: emails, proposals, marketing copy → Both Claude and ChatGPT do this well; reviewers give Claude the edge on longer pieces.[1]

The one rule that applies to all four tools

Read every output before it goes anywhere. Every time, without exception, especially at the start. These tools are useful, but they also make confident-sounding mistakes. A wrong figure in a client proposal or a tone-deaf customer email can cost more time to fix than it saved to generate. Build a quick review habit from day one.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the free versions for my business?

Yes, but with real limits. Free versions cap how much you can use the tool, restrict access to the most capable models, and can use your conversations for training unless you change a setting.[3] For occasional tasks, free works fine. For consistent business use across a team, a business plan is more practical and comes with better data protections.

Do I need to tell my customers I used AI to write something?

Rules vary by industry and location, and some professional fields (legal, financial, healthcare) have specific requirements. Check with your industry association or a lawyer if you're unsure. Whatever the rule, make sure the output is accurate before it goes out under your name.

What if the AI gives me wrong information?

It will, eventually. All four tools are capable of stating incorrect facts confidently. For anything you plan to act on (pricing, legal terms, medical information, historical facts), verify it independently before you use it. Treat AI output the way you'd treat a suggestion from a smart intern on their first day: useful input that still needs your review.

Can I run more than one of these tools at the same time?

Yes. One workable setup: Copilot or Gemini for tasks inside the office apps you already use, plus ChatGPT or Claude for writing tasks that benefit from a focused conversation outside them. You don't need to pick one forever.

Bottom line

The software you already use is the fastest path to getting real use out of AI. Microsoft 365 shop? Copilot. Google Workspace shop? Gemini. Neither? Start with ChatGPT's free version, build one habit around it, then upgrade when you've outgrown it. And if contract review or long-document work is a pain point in your business right now, try Claude for that job.

None of these tools is hard to start. The one you don't start is the only one that can't help you.
